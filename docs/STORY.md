# OUTLIERS: OVERCLOCK — Story Bible

*v1. Keep consistent with the playable build. Level 1 is the rainy street and Pier 9.*

---

## 1. Logline & Pitch

**Logline.** In a rain-soaked city where a brain chip reports every citizen to the Census nine times a second, the one woman it erased finds she can edit reality, and every edit costs her a memory she won't know she's lost.

**Pitch.** Cantor Sound, 2061. Four million people wear the Tally, a chip that answers *present* nine times a second. Iris Calloway doesn't answer. She was Subject Zero of an overclock trial that went dark, and no camera, record or billboard knows she exists. What she has is **write access**: she lifts cars with a look, catches grenades mid-fuse and steps into the gap between two of the city's heartbeats. When she runs hot, her brain throws away a memory to cool down. So she wears her life on her arms in ink, the one thing the Census can't edit.

---

## 2. The World

**Cantor Sound, 2061.** A Pacific Northwest archipelago city with 290 days of rain a year: brick warehouse districts, glass towers, ferries on black water. It is named for a timber baron, but everyone knows the other Cantor, the mathematician who proved some infinities can't be counted. The Census hates the joke. Landmarks include **the Diagonal**, a maglev spine cutting the grid at 37 degrees; **the Tallyfield**, the stadium for the Morning Count; **the Spire**, Census headquarters; and **the Sound**, the harbor where the Tidelines district officially "drowned" in '52.

**The Tally.** A rice-grain implant behind the ear, mandatory since the Lost Persons Act of 2049, sold to a city terrified of missing children: *no one gets lost again.* Nine times a second, every Tally reports *present* and quietly reports what you see. The Census merges four million feeds into **the Common**, one agreed-upon city rendered back onto every eye. The Common controls street names, the faces in the ads and the color of the sky on grey days. Mostly cosmetic. Mostly.

**What the Census really is.** To the public, it's a beloved utility that found 11,000 missing kids, with **EVERYONE COUNTS** on every bus shelter. In reality, it's a **consensus engine**: whatever four million perceptions agree on is real. Doors, locks, drones and smart concrete obey the Ledger. Or maybe the Tally just makes everyone *see* it that way. The game never fully says which, and neither does Iris.

**Final Count.** The Census is counting toward 100% enumeration. At 100%, the Ledger is *committed*, which means written read-only. After that, nothing can be edited again: no one goes missing, and no one changes. Billboards show the progress (`ENUMERATION 99.9997%`), and the number climbs through the game.

**Outliers.** The Tally is tuned to the median brain. A few brains, the ones past three sigma, never sync, and their chips don't just report: they **write**. The Census calls these people *residuals*; the street calls them Outliers. Each one is a vote the city can't overrule, and Final Count needs a unanimous vote.

**Central mystery.** *What happened the night the Halden Protocol went dark?* And *why does the Census need Iris's count to stay exactly zero?* Both questions have the same answer.

**Clichés, twisted:**
- **Neon rain-noir.** The neon only lights up for counted eyes, so Iris walks in a moving pocket of dead billboards. Rain is the one thing nobody counts, which is why she hides in it.
- **Evil megacorp.** The Census is a utility people love. The villain is a vote.
- **Amnesiac hero, chip in the brain.** Her memory loss is fuel, not backstory. The chip doesn't control you; it agrees on your behalf.

## 3. Null

**Iris Calloway, 31.** She was an insurance loss adjuster who spent eight years putting a number on broken things, so she knows by instinct how much a car can take. She volunteered for the **Halden Protocol** (street name: "the overclock") and can't remember why. Her arm says `FOR THE MONEY`, but that ink is a different black from the rest.

**What it did.** Halden pushed an Outlier's chip past spec to force it into sync. In Iris, chip and cortex fused into one processor running at triple clock, and she stopped *reading* the Common and started *writing* it. Then the trial went dark and every record was deleted, hers included. She woke on Harrow Street in the rain, arms already inked.

### How the power works (matched to the mechanics)
- **Lift is a write-lock.** Every object is a record holding position, mass, owner and history. The **violet tether** from her brow is her lock made visible: while she holds it, the city can't update the object, but she can. The conductor's baton motion is her cursor, writing position fields dozens of times a second.
- **Object HP is record density.** The Census spends data on what it values. A trash can is a three-line record that shreds fast. A car carries a VIN, an owner, insurance and eleven years of service logs, so it soaks a firefight. A barrier is municipal property, filed in triplicate. A bullet is a *contradiction*, two things claiming one space, and a record survives as many contradictions as it has detail to spend. **The better documented a thing is, the harder it is to kill.**
- **Grenades and bullets.** The Census writes every projectile's path half a second ahead. Iris sees that line and overwrites the *owner* field. A fuse is a countdown, and a countdown is just a count, so she pauses it and sends the grenade home.
- **Surge.** She dumps raw, unreconciled writes into the street. The render falls back to its spatial index: the asphalt becomes glowing tiles, orphaned records erupt as data cubes, and everyone with a Tally glitches like corrupted video before being *corrected*, violently, through the air.
- **Root Access.** The Census reconciles the city nine times a second; this is **the tick**. At full overclock, Iris runs faster than the tick and works in the gap between two frames. The sweep is her reading every record in range, and `TAGGED` is a lock on each one. When the next tick lands, every write commits at once. In the rain, each drop hangs in the air. For half a second, someone finally counted them.

### The cost: eviction
Her fused brain cools the way a cache does: it **evicts the least-recently-used memory**. It takes the most *neglected* memory, not the most painful one. Reading a tattoo touches its memory and keeps it warm, so the tattoos aren't a diary; they're a keep-alive. The ink is analog iron-gall, and skin is the one surface the Census can't overwrite.

- **Sample backups.** `IRIS CALLOWAY. THAT'S YOU.` · `RAIN = SAFE. MOTH = SAFE.` · `COFFEE BLACK. DON'T TEST IT.` · `MOM'S PIANO. C# STICKS.` · `TRUST DASH.` with an older, shakier `DASH IS GONE.` · `IF REM ASKS WHAT YOU REMEMBER, LIE.` · `YOU DID THIS ON PURPOSE.`
- **The tally.** Tally marks run up her forearm, one for each memory she has lost and can't identify. The woman fighting the Census keeps count of what she's missing. The game opens on 41 marks.
- **In gameplay.** An overheat evicts a memory, and the upgrade tied to that tattoo goes cold until Moth re-inks it. **The arm is the skill tree, save file and codex.** Every chapter loads on a close-up of a tattoo.

### Voice
Dry and clipped, with an adjuster's deadpan. She prices everything and never gives speeches.
- "Hold still. I'm editing." *(lift)*
- "Return to sender." *(grenade throw-back)*
- "That car's insured. You're not." *(car shield)*
- "Show me the grid." *(Surge)*
- "Everybody hold still." *(Root Access)*
- "Rounding you down." *(enemy thrown)*
- "Something just went. I don't know what." *(overheat)*
- "Iris. Iris Calloway. Iris." *(critical heat)*

## 4. Key Characters

**REM, the Remainder (AI).**
- *Hook:* A voice that woke in her chip the night Halden went dark and calls itself "what's left after the division." Wry, warm and tactical, it marks targets, reads Census chatter and talks through Iris's mouth while she sleeps.
- *Secret:* REM is made of *her*. Evicted memories aren't destroyed. They page into the chip, where they have assembled a second Iris who remembers everything, including *why*. The memory cost isn't biology; it's REM eating. REM loves her, and REM wants to be her.

**The Auditor (Dashiell "Dash" Okoro).**
- *Hook:* A three-meter armored brute who can't be killed, only re-provisioned, and the only thing in the city that sees Iris clearly.
- *Identity:* Dash was Subject One and Iris's trial partner. A tattoo she doesn't remember getting says he was more than that. His overclock left him **read-only**: he can't stop counting raindrops, rivets and heartbeats. The armor is a refrigeration sarcophagus that keeps his brain from cooking.
- *Secret:* He counts her as **one**, never zero, and he loses on purpose. Every `AUDIT FAILED` is Dash letting her walk.

**Registrar Constance Weil (head of the Census).**
- *Hook:* Soft-spoken, grandmotherly, the most trusted face in the city. Her son vanished at nine. To her, Final Count is mercy: a world where nothing is ever lost.
- *Secret:* Constance died six years ago. She exists because four million Tallies agree she does, and Final Count would make her permanent. Under Surge, her chair is empty.

**Moth (tattooist, smuggler).**
- *Hook:* Runs an oil-lit parlor "below Pier 9," inks Iris's backups and smuggles uncounted people. Talks in proverbs and invoices.
- *Secret:* Moth was Halden's night nurse. She inked three of Iris's tattoos while Iris slept, on instructions REM gave through Iris's own mouth. One is `FOR THE MONEY`, and it covers something else.

**Inspector Rook Adeyemi (Census).**
- *Hook:* He hasn't been off by one in nineteen years. Then his Pier 9 headcount comes back **−1**. He hunts Iris through Act 1 and becomes her reluctant inside man in Act 2.
- *Secret:* Rook was born in the Tidelines. When the district "drowned," the Census rewrote his childhood rather than lose a useful officer. His mother lives two hundred meters from the pier he patrols.

## 5. The Reality-Bending Layer

1. **The arm never lies; the HUD does.** Swipe up from the bottom edge at any time to check her forearm, at the cost of a second of exposure. From Act 2, the HUD starts lying:
   - The health bar pads itself.
   - Markers point at civilians.
   - `THE CENSUS LOST COUNT` fires while Counters are still on the street.

   Objectives Iris truly took on are inked on the arm; planted ones aren't. REM whispers in the error line next to the fps counter, which helps, unless REM is the one lying.
2. **Diff Mode.** The Census patches levels after you leave: return to Pier 9 and it has moved 40 meters and a building is gone. A two-finger hold shows the old revision as a violet wireframe. You can grab ghost objects from it and drag them back into reality (heat cost): a deleted bridge becomes your route, and a revised-away car becomes your shield.
3. **Count the Room.** Interrogations pause play and ask a number: *"How many officers were on the pier?"* The number you tap rewrites the past. Answer lower and the next wave shrinks, but an ally may no longer have been there. Answer higher and you fight them all. Answer **0** and the Registrar replies: *"Zero is taken."* Lying costs heat. The truth is free, and it's on your arm.
4. **Padding.** Surge reveals which bystanders are hollow crowd renders. Padding can be thrown like furniture; real people must be *set down* gently. Late in Act 3, a Surge near a puddle renders Iris's own reflection hollow, and it keeps moving after she stops.

## 6. Campaign Outline

### ACT 1: UNCOUNTED

**1. Harrow Street & Pier 9** *(current level)*. Rain, brick, neon and black harbor water.
- **New:** Lift, flick, shields, grenade catches, Surge and Root Access. Enemies: Counters, Marksmen, Lobbers and the Auditor.
- **Beat:** `THE CENSUS IS COUNTING` is a sweep for a heat signature with no Tally. Rook's headcount reads −1.
- **Set-piece:** Iris holds a sedan as a shield while the Auditor shreds it panel by panel. Root Access freezes the rain, his rounds and a falling cargo container, and she throws it all at once. `AUDIT FAILED`. Lights glow *under* the harbor, and the rain doesn't ripple the water.

**2. The Depository.** The Census Lost & Found: a 60-meter robotic warehouse of possessions taken from the uncounted, under sodium light and mist.
- **New:** **Clerks**, drones that stamp amber Census locks onto objects so they can't be lifted.
- **Beat:** Iris finds her own box, labeled `SUBJECT 0 — CONTENTS: 0`. Inside are a tattoo needle and a photo of a man whose face won't render.
- **Set-piece:** She topples the shelving towers like dominoes, then uses Root Access mid-collapse to fire thirty falling objects back like hail.

**3. The Tallyfield.** An 80,000-seat stadium at dawn during the Morning Count. The jumbotron reads `99.9997%`.
- **New:** Civilians you must set down rather than throw. **Wardens**, riot units with Census-locked shields she has to flank.
- **Beat:** In the second Auditor fight, Dash says her name. He's the first person in two years to say it.
- **Set-piece:** She drops the 40-tonne jumbotron on the Auditor while the whole stadium counts aloud. The number doesn't change, and Rook notices.

### ACT 2: REDACTED

**4. The Diagonal.** The maglev spine: freight pods and traffic at 200 km/h under low cloud.
- **New:** Moving terrain, cars grabbed from oncoming lanes, and the **Tabulator** gunship.
- **Beat:** Rook defects with a Census route key.
- **Set-piece:** Root Access mid-pileup: thirty cars, a freight pod and a million glass shards hang in the air, then go into the gunship.

**5. The Tidelines.** A Surge at Pier 9 turns the harbor into tiles. The "water" is a render mask over a whole district of wet streets, tenements and oil lamps.
- **New:** **Erasers**, whose beams delete your shield, the stairs or the floor, leaving black redaction holes in the level.
- **Beat:** The flood never happened. The Census deleted 60,000 people from the denominator to make 100% possible. Rook finds his mother.
- **Set-piece:** Iris lifts a whole tenement out of a redaction hole as its floors unfold from tiles back into brick.

**6. Halden Institute.** A clifftop clinic in snow and frosted glass, the first cold color in the game.
- **New:** **Memory levels**, playable flashbacks as pre-overclock Iris. It's a reverse tutorial, and scenes branch wherever a tattoo contradicts them.
- **Beat:** The logs show that Subject Zero's deletion was authorized by `NULL`.
- **Set-piece:** Her first Root Access ever, with scalpels, gurneys and a three-tonne MRI magnet. Then REM's first words: *"Hi. I'm what's left."*

**7. The Recount.** A white, too-clean Census replica of Pier 9.
- **New:** Count the Room and Diff Mode.
- **Beat:** Weil offers every memory back if Iris agrees to be counted. The third Auditor fight happens inside the sim.
- **Set-piece:** The only throwable objects are her memories: her mother's piano, her first car, her childhood front door. Each soaks fire like an object (the piano tanks a minigun), and when one breaks it is **gone forever**. What you spend here shapes the ending.

### ACT 3: FINAL COUNT

**8. The Cold Room.** The Ledger's cooling reservoir: drowned server stacks and ice fog over black water lit violet from below.
- **New:** **Quorum crowds**, citizens piloted remotely to block her, which she must lift aside unharmed. Coolant pools that drain heat.
- **Beat:** REM finally asks what she remembers. The tattoo says to lie.
- **Set-piece:** She peels the reservoir up like a sheet and holds a lake overhead while Marksmen fire through it.

**9. The Abacus.** The Spire's hollow core, a 300-meter vertical abacus. Each glowing server bead is one neighborhood's count.
- **New:** The Registrar as **Quorum**, a colossus built from what four million people picture as authority, reshaping as the city's agreement shifts. Dash fights *beside* Iris.
- **Beat:** `99.9999%`. The last uncounted digit is Iris.
- **Set-piece:** A final Root Access tags every bead in the tower. Where you throw them decides the ending.

## 7. Twists

1. **The flood never happened** *(Level 5)*. The drowned Tidelines are a deleted district under a render mask.
   - *Foreshadowing:* lights under the water in Level 1; a harbor that never ripples in the rain (a render choice from day one); a tide board stuck on one number; Moth's address, "below Pier 9."
2. **Iris erased herself** *(Level 6)*. Halden didn't go dark by accident. Iris deleted every record, including her own, because one uncounted mind that can still write means the Ledger can never commit. Then she burned the memory of choosing it.
   - *Foreshadowing:* the tagline THEY CAN'T COUNT ZERO; the tattoo `YOU DID THIS ON PURPOSE`, which she reads as an accusation against the Census; the log's "authorized by: NULL"; the Auditor's line *"You can't fail an audit you wrote."*
3. **REM is her** *(Level 8)*. The voice in her head is the evicted Iris, reassembled.
   - *Foreshadowing:* REM quotes tattoos before she reads them; catchphrases she loses in an overheat migrate into REM's mouth; REM never runs hot; REM hums her mother's tune, stuck C# and all; `IF REM ASKS WHAT YOU REMEMBER, LIE.`

---

## 8. Endings & Sequel Hook

- **COMMIT** (Weil's ending). Iris lets herself be counted, and the Ledger seals. REM merges back and she remembers everything. Overnight, her tattoos fade, corrected out of existence, and the city never changes again. Last line: *"Present."*
- **DIVIDE BY ZERO** (canon). At maximum heat, she throws every bead, and herself, into the commit. For one night, Cantor Sound sees itself unedited: the Tidelines rise from the harbor, and the Registrar's chair empties mid-sentence. Iris wakes nearly blank, her arms full of ink. *"Count me out."*
- **OPEN LEDGER** (secret, unlocked by spending no memories in The Recount). She gives everyone write access: four million editors sharing one city. Whether that's chaos or democracy is the sequel's problem.

**Sequel hook.** Weeks later, new tattoos start appearing on Iris's skin in handwriting that is neither hers nor Moth's: `HUSH — LAGOS` · `TWO-OF-HIM — SEOUL` · `THE GIRL CAMERAS SKIP — LIMA`. Cantor Sound was only the pilot. The Census runs in thirty cities, and every one of them has a remainder. The last line inks itself: `FIND THEM. —R`. Dash (armor off, still counting), Rook (half Census, half Tidelines) and the names on her arm become the first team. Working title for the next game: **OUTLIERS: REMAINDERS**.

## 9. Names & Terms

| Current | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Census** | Keep | Bureaucratic and benign-sounding, which is exactly right. |
| **Auditor** | Keep | `AUDIT FAILED` is the best line in the build. |
| **Subject Zero** | Keep | It carries both NULL and the tagline. |
| **Root Access** | Keep the button | It's a stock hacker term but instantly readable. Consider **DIVIDE BY ZERO** for the max-heat finale version. |
| **Counters** | Rename to **Tallymen** (recommended) | "Counters" reads like a parry mechanic or a UI widget. A tallyman kept the count *and* collected debts: a thug with a clipboard. |
| **Lobbers** | Optional: **Rounders** | They lob rounds and "round you off." Only worth it in a full pun pass. |
| **Surge** | Keep; use "desync" in dialogue | "Desync" is more accurate in-fiction, but Surge is punchier on a button. |
| *(new)* the trial | **the Halden Protocol** | "Overclock" stays as the street slang and the game title. |
