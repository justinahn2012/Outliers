# OUTLIERS: Overclock — Story v2 (tight)

*Canonical. The longer first draft is in `STORY_v1.md`; where they disagree, this wins.*

## The spine

**Premise.** In Cantor Sound, reality is whatever four million chipped minds agree on. Iris Calloway is the one mind that can disagree out loud, and every time she does, she loses a memory.

**The question the whole game asks:** who decides what's real, everyone or the one who remembers?

**Two people who are both right:**
- **Iris** wants the Tidelines back: 60,000 people the Census deleted so the count could reach 100%. A consensus built by erasing the people who disagreed isn't a consensus.
- **Registrar Weil** wants Final Count: a city where nothing and no one is ever lost again. Iris is one person breaking reality for four million who chose it.

Every scene should push on that question. If a scene doesn't, cut it.

**The answer the game gives is the twist: Iris decides, and she decides everyone goes.** The Census erased 60,000 people so four million could agree. Iris erases four million so the 60,000 can come back. She becomes the Census run backwards, and the count finally reaches 100%, because there is one person left to agree.

## Five rules

| # | Rule | What it means | What it lets us do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Agreement is real.** | The consensus is the whole city. Every chip honors the Ledger, so whatever it records is true for all four million. The Census changes reality by agreement: it issues a record, every chip accepts it, it becomes real. Slow, free, needs everyone. | The Census can create walls, erase districts and keep dead people alive by issuing records the city accepts. |
| 2 | **Iris writes directly.** | Halden fused her chip and brain: she writes any record instantly, with no one's agreement. Dash came out read-only: he perceives everything exactly and can change nothing. | No limit on what she can edit, only on what she can afford. |
| 3 | **Every edit costs, and bigger costs more.** | Edits make heat (her focus bar); heat evicts memories. A trash can costs almost nothing. Deleting something the whole city honors (a wall, a building, a person) costs a memory she'd notice: a name, a face. Evicted memories collect in her chip and became REM. | Small edits are her weapons. Big edits are sacrifices. |
| 4 | **The consensus pushes back.** | If she edits something the city still expects, the Census agrees it straight back. An edit sticks only once nothing in the consensus still needs the old version. Every person who accepts her edit weakens the push. | She can't just delete her problems; she has to make the old version unnecessary first. Witnesses like Rook are cracks in the consensus. |
| 5 | **Ink can't be edited.** | Nothing analog is in the Ledger: skin, paper, iron-gall ink. | The tattoos are ground truth. Anything that contradicts her arm is a lie. |

**Two ways to change reality.** The Census writes by agreement: slow, free, needs everyone. Iris writes directly: instant, needs no one, costs her memories. In Chapter 1 she can only afford small edits, so she wins by making the Census's own records lapse. By Act 3 she's choosing which memories to spend on edits big enough to change the city.

## Three layers of reality

| Layer | What can change it | Anchor (can't lie) | How it plays |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The city** (Rules 1, 4) | The Census, and Iris | Analog things: ink, paper, Moth's parlor | Streets rearrange; her edits get agreed back; people who exist by agreement |
| **Her memory** (Rules 3, 5) | Eviction, and REM | The tattoos | Levels that contradict her arm; skills go cold when their memory is evicted |
| **The replay** (the game) | REM, and the player | Moth's chair: the present | Levels that change on replay; the AGAIN button; the final reveal |

**Writing rule:** break one layer at a time and leave its anchor intact. Act 1 bends the city, Act 2 adds her memory, Act 3 stacks all three. Complexity comes from stacking layers, not new rules.

## The frame

The whole game is played from Moth's chair. Iris has evicted so much that she's losing herself; each chapter is a memory REM replays so it isn't lost (Rule 3: using a memory keeps it). Moth inks what survives. The arm is the menu between memories; the parlor is the hub.

Clues already in the build: rain in every level that never ripples the water (`RAIN = SAFE`); the **AGAIN** button and "Not like this. Not again."; props shifting between retries; every chapter ends with Moth: "You're late. Sit. Arm."

REM is searching the replays for the one memory Iris burned: the reason she erased herself, which is also the only thing that can stop Final Count for good. The Census wants it, REM wants it, and Iris doesn't know she's looking for it.

| **3 · Final Count** | + Replay | The Recount · The Cold Room · The Abacus | She's racing Weil to 100%. | None of it was now: every chapter was a replay in Moth's chair. The rain stops, the present begins, and the city is empty. She reached 100% first, the only way it could be reached. |

**Endings** (all three are chosen in the empty present, from Moth's chair):
- **COMMIT:** keep the edit. Write the 60,000 Tidelines back into a city that has room for them, and be the only one who remembers the four million. "The count is one."
- **DIVIDE BY ZERO:** spend every memory, REM included, to write the four million back. They return with no memory of being gone; she doesn't return. "Count me out."
- **Secret, LET REM:** give her body to the Iris who remembers everything, including choosing it. REM would do it again.

## The final twist: the count is one

**Iris is the one who wipes out the population.** Not the Census, not REM. She decides.

**Why the rules make her do it.**
- Rule 4 means she can never win an argument with four million chips. Whatever she writes, they agree back. Chapter 1 teaches this in play: walls heal, cordons come back "RENEWED ×6", and the city writes every edit back.
- Weil's Final Count would lock the Ledger at 100%, with the Tidelines deleted forever.
- An edit sticks only once nothing needs the old version. The only edit with no pushback is one with no witnesses left. So she makes the city quiet: she deletes every record in the Ledger, and everyone who exists by agreement stops existing.
- Then, with no one left to disagree, the Tidelines can be written back. But every big edit costs memories, and she has spent nearly all of hers. That is why she's in Moth's chair.

**Who's left, and why.**
- **Moth:** he never had a chip, so he was never a record (Rule 5).
- **REM:** it lives in her chip.
- **Dash:** he always counted her as one. Now the count is one.

**The frame, re-read.** The game is played in the present, in an empty Cantor Sound. The rain that never ripples the water is the replay; when it stops, you see the streets with no one in them. Every Census trooper, every caged civilian and the informant were records: replays of people who no longer exist. Moth isn't helping her escape. He's inking what's left of her so she remembers what she did, and he's making her walk back through it until she finds the moment she chose.

**The burned memory.** REM has been searching for one memory: the moment she decided. It's the memory she burned. "Don't worry, Iris. I've got it." (the end of every chapter) is what REM said when she asked it to run the final edit. The player's hands were on it the whole time: every lift and every rewrite was practice for the last one.

**Seeds already in Chapter 1.**
- The Auditor going down: "...One."
- A fragment of Dash: "'One,' he says. 'I count you as one.'"
- A fragment: "If you're hearing this, I chose this."
- A fragment of Dash counting raindrops and stopping at zero.
- A fragment of the last ferry to Halden: "You came back alone."
- The first time she rewrites a checkpoint, REM: "Then we'll need a quieter city."
- "COUNT: 1" sprayed on a shuttered shop: the present leaking into the replay.
- Moth: "You're late. Sit. Arm."

**Writing rule for the twist.** Never state it before Act 3. Every seed must read as something else on a first pass: a number, a joke, a sad memory.


## Chapter 1: why everything is there

Iris's goal: reach Moth's, below Pier 9, before she forgets the way. Everything in the chapter is the Census trying to stop her, and every obstacle works by the rules.

| Element | What it is | Why it works that way |
|---|---|---|
| **The count sweep** | The Census sweeps the district for a heat signature with no Tally, sealing it into cells. | Rule 1: Final Count can't finish while she's out there. |
| **Checkpoints** (amber walls) | Cordon orders in the Ledger; every chip in the city honors them, so they're solid. A cordon only stands while it's renewed, by officers on station or through a relay. | She could delete one, but it would cost a memory she'd miss (Rule 3), and while it's renewed the city agrees it straight back (Rule 4). So she clears the officers or cuts the relay and the order lapses. |
| **The two that never open** | Market arch and Gut Lane→Pier 9, renewed by whole garrisons on the far side. | Too expensive to delete, impossible to lapse from her side; she detours. They lapse when the sweep is called off. |
| **Tally relays** | The Census uplink that renews every cordon on a block. | Edit a relay and the city agrees it back (Rule 4); a thrown car is a real object, so smashing works. |
| **Warden shields** | Each carries its own lock order: no grip through it. | Surge floods the order with raw writes and it lapses; a broken shield is ordinary matter she can use. |
| **Clerks** | Stamp temporary lock orders into objects. | Orders expire; Clerks are light enough to throw. |
| **Cold Storage** | Moth's uncounted people, tagged for Halden; the protocol is still running. | No chips (Rule 1): once out, the Census can't follow. |
| **The lockdown** | The Census pins her in the rail yard. | The 90-second timer is the Lamplighter's arrival time. |
| **The Lamplighter** | Searchlight drone; its beam counts by sight, chip or no chip. | Rule 1: held long enough, it writes her into the count and calls troops. |
| **The informant** | Sold Moth's address; running to report it in person. | Catching him keeps Moth hidden; Rook's tip turns him from witness to ally (Rule 4). |
| **The Auditor's retreat** | At half strength he walks into the sea, returns on the ferry pier. | Rule 2: read-only, he can't stop counting and runs hot; his armor is a freezer and the sea cools him. |
| **The ferry pier** | Moth's door is in the old ferry terminal; the Auditor stands between her and it. | The last obstacle is the one person who counts her as one. |
| **Her powers** | Lift and catch from the start. REM hands back Surge at the plaza relays, Rewrite in the tram cut, and Root Access during the lockdown. | Powers are memories she lost; REM gives them back when she needs them (Rule 3, and REM's own agenda). |
| **Catching bullets** | Touch a round in the air and it stops; let go and it goes home to whoever fired it, or flick it somewhere else. | A bullet is a tiny record that nothing else needs, so stopping it costs almost nothing (Rules 2 and 3). |
| **Rewrite: walls and street** | Hold on a wall and a piece of it tears out for her to throw; hold on the street and it stands up as cover. | Rule 2: she can write any record. Rule 4: a wall is still needed by every chip that has ever seen it, so within seconds the city agrees it back: the gap closes, the torn piece was never there, and the cover sinks. Rule 3: each edit costs heat. |
| **Rewriting a checkpoint** | She can write a cordon open, and it flickers. | Every officer on station renews it in the same instant, so it's written straight back ("RENEWED ×6"). Clear them and it lapses for good. |
| **Memory fragments** | Her evicted memories surfacing in the replay; picking one up takes it back from REM. | Rule 3 + the frame ("Oh. That one was in my pile. Keep it."). |
| **"Cooling down", XP, the arm** | She is literally overheating; each level is a memory Moth can ink. | Rules 3 and 5. |

**Rook's arc:** curious at the plaza → witness at the rail yard → ally at the container yard.
